## What's new

This is the first release of Pomodot, a small Pomodoro timer for macOS.

- **Menu bar timer.** Pomodot lives in the menu bar with the time left next to its icon. Its menu has every control: Start, Pause, Skip, Reset and New Cycle.
- **Floating Window.** A little glass capsule floats above your other apps. Click it to open a card with the timer and its buttons. Drag it anywhere, hide it, or switch off Always on Top.
- **Your own timings.** Set the Focus, Short Break and Long Break lengths and the number of Pomodoros per Cycle. They're 25 / 5 / 15 / 4 to begin with. A change takes effect right away, with no Save button.
- **Banner and sound.** When a Phase runs out, you get a banner and a sound. Pick any system sound for the end of a Focus and the end of a Break, or None.
- **Auto-start.** Breaks, and Focus after a Short Break, can start by themselves.
- **Expected finish.** See when the current Phase and the whole Cycle will end.
- **Statistics.** Today, this week, all time, and a chart of the last 7 days.
- **Appearance.** Light, Dark, or follow your Mac.

Requires macOS 14 Sonoma or later, on Apple silicon or Intel.

## Install or update

Download **Pomodot-0.9.0.zip** below and drag **Pomodot** into Applications. Pomodot isn't sold through Apple, so the first time you open it, macOS asks you to confirm it once in System Settings › Privacy & Security › **Open Anyway**. That takes about a minute.

When you update to a later version, drag the new Pomodot over the old one. macOS asks you to confirm it again with Open Anyway.

Step-by-step instructions, in English and Thai, are in the [README](https://github.com/inwpasit619/pomodot#readme).

## Your data is kept

Your History and Settings are stored apart from the app. When you drag a newer Pomodot over this one, everything stays. Deleting the app keeps it too, so it's all there if you install Pomodot again. The README explains how to remove it completely.
